Background

Why can the gut microbiota change over time?

Diet, age, medication use, illness, and lifestyle can all influence the composition of the gut microbiota. Diets low in fiber may also provide fewer prebiotic substrates for gut microorganisms.

Antibiotics can alter both beneficial and potentially harmful bacterial populations. Probiotic products should therefore be evaluated by strain identity, viable count, formulation, and suitability—not by a single number alone.

Probiotic Strains

The 14 cultures included in FloraUp®

Strains 1–7

Commonly used cultures in digestive-wellness formulas

  • Streptococcus thermophilus: A culture widely used in yogurt and other fermented dairy products.
  • Lactobacillus reuteri: A well-studied species found in the human gastrointestinal tract.
  • Bifidobacterium longum subsp. infantis: A Bifidobacterium associated with the early-life intestinal microbiota.
  • Bifidobacterium breve: A species commonly identified in the intestinal microbiota of breastfed infants.
  • Lactobacillus plantarum: A food-associated species selected for its tolerance of gastrointestinal conditions.
  • Lactobacillus paracasei: A culture commonly included in probiotic foods and supplements.
  • Lactobacillus rhamnosus: A species frequently selected for acid and bile tolerance.
Strains 8–14

Additional cultures used in multi-strain probiotic formulas

  • Lactobacillus casei: A culture widely used in fermented foods and probiotic products.
  • Lactobacillus fermentum: A species selected for tolerance of gastric acid and bile.
  • Lactobacillus acidophilus: A commonly used culture associated with lactose fermentation.
  • Lactobacillus salivarius: A species found in the oral cavity and gastrointestinal tract.
  • Lactobacillus helveticus: A lactic-acid culture widely used in fermented dairy production.
  • Bifidobacterium bifidum: A Bifidobacterium species commonly found in the human intestine.
  • Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus: A traditional yogurt culture that ferments lactose.
Suggested Use

Follow the product label and adjust use to individual needs

  • For general use, take 1–2 sachets with breakfast in warm water not exceeding 40°C.
  • The powder may be used as part of a consistent daily probiotic routine.
  • If you have questions about product suitability, consult a qualified healthcare professional.
Precautions

Review individual health needs before use

  • People with chronic conditions, serious illness, or ongoing medication use should consult a healthcare professional before taking supplements.
  • Suitable for lacto-ovo vegetarians. Review the product label for current testing and allergen information.
  • Probiotics should complement, not replace, a balanced diet and healthy lifestyle.
